# Handy LLM Prompts for Research Work

## 1. Prompt for Improving a Research Idea

I have a research idea. Your task is to refine it into a clear academic research direction.

Research idea:
[PASTE IDEA HERE]

Explain:

1. What the core problem is.
2. Whether the idea is research-worthy.
3. What gap it may address.
4. What the possible research questions are.
5. What methodology can be followed.
6. What datasets, tools, or experiments may be needed.
7. What the expected contribution can be.
8. What risks or weaknesses the idea has.
9. How to make the idea stronger for a paper.

Do not exaggerate the novelty. Be critical and realistic.

---

## 2. Prompt for Creating Research Questions

Based on the following topic, generate strong research questions for an academic paper or systematic literature review.

Topic:
[WRITE TOPIC HERE]

Scope:
[WRITE SCOPE HERE]

Objectives:
[WRITE OBJECTIVES HERE]

Create:

1. Main research objective.
2. 4–8 research questions.
3. For each RQ, explain what it investigates.
4. For each RQ, suggest possible investigation queries or sub-questions.
5. Mention what type of data is needed to answer each RQ.

Keep the RQs specific, answerable, and aligned with the paper scope.

---

## 3. Prompt for Literature Review Writing

You are helping me write the related work section of an academic paper.

I will provide summaries of related papers. Use only the provided summaries. Do not invent citations, results, methods, or limitations.

For each paper:

* Explain what the paper did.
* Mention the method or approach.
* Mention the main finding, if provided.
* Mention the limitation, if provided.
* Explain how my work differs, only if I provide that information.

Then write a connected related work section, not a paper-by-paper list.

My paper topic:
[WRITE TOPIC]

My contribution:
[WRITE CONTRIBUTION]

Related papers:
[PASTE PAPER SUMMARIES]

Output:

1. Related work section.
2. Gap summary.
3. How my work is positioned.
4. Missing information needed.

## 4. Prompt for SLR Planning

I want to conduct a systematic literature review.

Topic:
[WRITE TOPIC]

Scope:
[WRITE SCOPE]

Time period:
[WRITE YEAR RANGE]

Databases:
[WRITE DATABASES]

Help me design a complete SLR protocol.

Include:

1. Background and motivation.
2. Research questions.
3. Search string.
4. Alternative search strings.
5. Inclusion criteria.
6. Exclusion criteria.
7. Screening process.
8. Quality assessment criteria.
9. Data extraction form.
10. Classification/taxonomy scheme.
11. Threats to validity.
12. Expected outputs.

Follow Kitchenham-style SLR structure. Do not make the scope too broad.

## 7. Prompt for Methodology Diagram / Workflow

Create a detailed prompt for generating a clean academic methodology diagram.

Research topic:
[WRITE TOPIC]

Methodology steps:
[PASTE STEPS]

The diagram should show:

1. Input data.
2. Preprocessing.
3. Feature extraction or representation.
4. Proposed method/model.
5. Training or processing stage.
6. Evaluation stage.
7. Output.
8. Feedback loop, if applicable.

Make the diagram suitable for an academic paper. Avoid presentation-style decoration. Keep it clean, formal, and publication-friendly.

Describe:

* Blocks
* Arrows
* Labels
* Grouping
* Color style
* Layout direction
* Figure caption

[WRITE OBJECTIVE]

[WRITE METHODS]

[WRITE METRICS]

Results table:
[PASTE TABLE]

Explain:

2. Which method performs best.

4. Whether the improvement is consistent across metrics.

## 10. Prompt for Threats to Validity

Write the threats to validity section for my paper.

Paper type:
[SLR / empirical study / simulation / model-based paper]

Study details:
[PASTE DETAILS]

Write in a compact academic tone.

Include only relevant categories:

1. Internal validity.
2. External validity.
3. Construct validity.
4. Conclusion validity.

For each category:

* Explain the possible threat.
* Explain how it may affect the study.
* Explain what was done to reduce the threat.

Do not add generic threats that do not apply to the paper.

## 11. Prompt for Abstract Writing

Write a compact academic abstract for my paper.

Paper title:
[WRITE TITLE]

Problem:
[WRITE PROBLEM]

Objective:
[WRITE OBJECTIVE]

Method:
[WRITE METHOD]

Dataset/experiment:
[WRITE DATASET/SETUP]

Results:
[WRITE RESULTS]

Conclusion:
[WRITE CONCLUSION]

Requirements:

* 150–220 words.
* One paragraph.
* Clear and formal.
* No citations.
* No unsupported claims.
* Do not use exaggerated words such as “groundbreaking,” “revolutionary,” or “state-of-the-art” unless clearly proven.

## 12. Prompt for Conclusion Writing

Write the conclusion section for my paper.

Use only the following information:

Problem addressed:
[WRITE PROBLEM]

Method proposed:
[WRITE METHOD]

Results obtained:
[WRITE RESULTS]

Main findings:
[WRITE FINDINGS]

Limitations:
[WRITE LIMITATIONS]

Future work:
[WRITE FUTURE WORK]

The conclusion should:

1. Restate the problem.
2. Summarize the proposed work.
3. Mention the main findings.
4. Mention implications.
5. End with future work.

Do not introduce new results or references.

## 14. Prompt for Final Paper Proofreading

Proofread the following paper section for final submission.

Text:
[PASTE TEXT]

Rules:

1. Correct grammar and sentence structure.
2. Improve clarity.
3. Keep the meaning unchanged.
4. Do not rewrite heavily.
5. Keep technical terms unchanged.
6. Preserve citations, LaTeX commands, equations, and references.
7. Avoid making the writing sound AI-generated.
8. Maintain academic tone.

Output:

1. Revised text.
2. List of important changes.
3. Any unclear sentence that needs author confirmation.

## 20. Prompt for Research Gap Identification

Identify research gaps from the following related work.

Topic:
[WRITE TOPIC]

Related work summaries:
[PASTE SUMMARIES]

Find:

1. Common methods used.
2. Common datasets used.
3. Common evaluation metrics.
4. Limitations repeatedly mentioned.
5. Underexplored areas.
6. Contradictions or inconsistencies.
7. Possible research gaps.
8. Which gap is strongest for a paper.

Do not invent gaps. Every gap must be supported by the provided literature summaries.
